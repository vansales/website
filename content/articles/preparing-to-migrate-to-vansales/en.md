Many companies worry that changing their sales system means lost data, disrupted routes or a team that won't use it. In practice, most problems come from too little preparation, not from the system itself. With a good plan, managers get reliable numbers from day one, and reps don't have to work in two systems any longer than necessary.

## 1. Be clear about the goal

Before you start, write down what you want to fix: missed orders, van stock that never matches, or month-end figures that take too long. A clear goal tells you which data to move first and lets you measure whether the switch paid off.

## 2. Clean up your existing data

The core data to prepare:

- **Customers**: shop name, address, contact number, and the route or rep who looks after them
- **Products**: code, name, unit and selling price
- **Promotions**: discounts, free goods or bundle prices, with their conditions, dates, and which shops or customer groups qualify
- **Team**: reps, supervisors and vehicles

This is a good moment to remove shops that have closed, merge duplicates and make sure product codes are the same everywhere.

Promotions tend to be scattered across spreadsheets, letters to shops and reps' memories, so gather them in one place, drop the ones that have expired, and write the conditions clearly enough that everyone reads them the same way.

Vansales provides templates and import tools for customer and product data, so anything you already keep in Excel is straightforward to bring in, and our team can review and import it with you.

## 3. Plan connections to your other systems

If you already run accounting software or an ERP, discuss it early, because it decides which way your data flows.

- **Which system is the master**: agree where customers, products and prices are edited, and let the other system follow, so there's never a second version
- **Which documents need to move**: orders, invoices, payments and credit notes, and in what form accounting needs them
- **How often**: as each document is created, or in one batch at the end of the day
- **Who handles mismatches**: someone must own checking and fixing them, rather than leaving them until month-end

On plans that include the Open API and webhooks, your IT team or ERP administrator can sync customers, orders and invoices as they change. If you're not sure how your systems should connect, our team can help you weigh the options. Test the connection during the pilot, not on go-live day.

## 4. Pick the go-live date and plan the training

Choose a quieter period, such as the start of a month or after a festival season, and avoid month-end closing or big promotions. Agree on the exact cut-off date for outstanding balances and stock, so no invoice ends up in both systems.

**Training is the most important part of any migration.** However good the system, it won't help if people aren't comfortable using it. Count back from go-live and block out training time for everyone involved: reps, drivers, supervisors and the back office. Hold it close enough to the date that people still remember it, and use your own customers and products rather than sample data.

## 5. Run a short pilot, then go live together

Have a small group of around 10–20 people use the system for real for a week or two. Pick people who are open to new tools, because they'll help teach everyone else. The pilot is there to find missing data and steps that need adjusting.

While the pilot runs, plan the day everyone switches together. In our experience, rolling out one team at a time looks safer but means running two systems side by side for a long time. Your back office has to enter, check and reconcile both at once, falls behind and makes far more mistakes. Teams end up working in different ways, totals can't be compared, and the rollout drags on and wears everyone out. Switching the whole company at once puts everyone on the same standard from day one.

## 6. Get your people ready

- **Hands-on training**: have reps create an invoice and print a real receipt on their own phones
- **Explain why you're changing**: faster closing, no double entry, no more calling the office to check stock. Teams accept change more easily when they see what's in it for them
- **Name a go-to person**: in the first week, someone should be on hand to answer questions right away
- **Check the equipment**: phones, receipt printers and mobile data should all be ready before day one. See our guides to [mobile internet plans](/resources/mobile-internet-for-field-sales) and [Bluetooth receipt printers](/resources/bluetooth-receipt-printers)

## 7. Check the results after go-live

During the first week, compare sales, cash and van stock against reality every day, so small gaps get fixed while they're still small. Once the numbers match consistently, retire the old system.

## Checklist before go-live

- A clear goal for what you want to fix
- Customer, product, price and promotion data reviewed and de-duplicated
- A cut-off date set for outstanding balances and stock
- A master system agreed, and the link to accounting or ERP tested
- A pilot completed, and one go-live date set for everyone
- Everyone involved, from reps and drivers to supervisors and the back office, trained and knows who to ask
- Phones, printers and mobile data ready
